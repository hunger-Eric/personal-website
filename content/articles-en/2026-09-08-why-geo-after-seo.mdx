## The short answer

A website can have solid SEO and still need GEO. SEO is the foundation for GEO. It helps crawlers reach a page, helps search engines index it, and gives them useful signals about what the page covers. GEO follows the same page into a newer discovery path, where a person asks an AI system a complete question and reads a composed answer before deciding which website to visit.

A company may already rank for relevant searches and remain absent from generated answers. Its page may be retrieved while its brand, audience, deliverables, and limitations remain too vague to support an accurate response. The company may even appear by name while the answer describes the wrong service.

Keyword rankings alone cannot explain these outcomes. GEO examines the path from public access and retrieval to the way information is selected, represented, and cited in an answer. It extends the work that SEO has already made possible.

## The foundation SEO already provides

Google says its established SEO practices remain relevant to AI Overviews and AI Mode. A page must be indexed and eligible to appear with a snippet in Google Search before it can appear as a supporting link in these AI features. [Google guidance for AI features](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)

Technical SEO therefore keeps its job. Crawl controls, stable HTML responses, internal links, clear titles, useful text, canonical URLs, and structured data that matches the visible page all affect whether content can enter the process at all.

Google's newer guide to generative search makes the relationship even clearer. Its generative features use the Search index and core ranking systems to retrieve current web pages. Accessible, reliable, people-first content remains useful. [Google guide to generative search](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)

An existing SEO foundation gives a GEO project somewhere sensible to begin. The pages, links, and search diagnostics already in place can be examined rather than rebuilt.

## GEO examines visibility inside the answer

Classic search usually presents a set of links. A generated response combines material before the reader reaches a website. The questions also tend to be longer.

A buyer may ask which customer-service system fits pre-sales enquiries, whether it can use an existing knowledge base, where sensitive data is processed, and who reviews a wrong answer. A page that only says “intelligent customer service solution” may establish a topic. It does not give an answer system enough information to make that comparison.

Google explains that AI Overviews and AI Mode can fan a question out into several related searches and use supporting pages across subtopics. One response may therefore need material about capabilities, implementation requirements, operating limits, and evidence. [Google guidance for AI features](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)

GEO adds work in three places. Brand and service entities need to be explicit. Important claims need nearby conditions and support. Teams also need to observe what a target platform retrieved and whether the resulting answer used it accurately.

The GEO paper published at KDD in 2024 framed content visibility within generative answers as something that could be measured and tested. Its experiments provide a research starting point. They do not guarantee citations or commercial results on today's platforms. [Original GEO paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735)

## Where a page can fall out of the path

Access comes first. OpenAI documents OAI-SearchBot, GPTBot, and ChatGPT-User separately. OAI-SearchBot supports search visibility, GPTBot concerns content that may be used to improve models, and ChatGPT-User handles visits initiated by users. Publishers can manage these uses separately. [OpenAI crawler documentation](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots)

The main text then has to be available. A service description that appears only after browser-side code runs may be incomplete on some retrieval paths. A successful HTTP response confirms that a server replied. It does not confirm that a platform indexed the useful text.

Retrieved content still needs a clear job. Ambiguous brand names, adjective-heavy product copy, unsupported case claims, and distant qualifications all make a passage harder to use when it is separated from the rest of the page.

The final answer creates another set of outcomes. Presence in a source list, an accurate service description, and a visit to the website are separate observations. Evidence for one cannot establish the other two.

## Signs that a GEO review is due

Some companies still need to finish their basic service pages. That work should come first. A GEO review becomes useful when several of these conditions appear.

- Important pages are indexed at stable URLs, yet they rarely appear as sources in AI search.
- The brand is confused with a generic phrase, another company, or another product.
- An answer names the company but cannot explain its audience, deliverables, data boundaries, or human review process.
- The website, product documentation, media coverage, and third-party profiles make conflicting claims about the same capability.
- The team publishes regularly without recording target questions, cited pages, or changes in answers.

These symptoms can come from technical access, content, or external evidence. Separating the layers helps a team spend effort on the actual gap.

## Begin with real buyer questions

A small GEO review can start with ten questions that buyers already ask. Sales calls, proposal discussions, and support enquiries usually provide better material than a new list of invented keywords.

Write each question at the decision level. Who is the service for? What data does implementation require? What will be delivered? Which actions still require human approval? How does recovery work? What evidence will count as acceptance? If the website cannot answer, publish reviewed facts first. If the answer is already on the page, check access and observe whether the target system uses the right passage.

Record the platform, language, test date, search mode, complete response, and cited URLs. Keep the questions stable for the next review. The resulting record supports comparison without turning one mention into a trend.

## Boundaries

This guide concerns public company websites and their visibility in search and generated answers. Platforms do not share one index, crawler policy, or answer process. Google's rules cannot stand in for OpenAI's rules or those of another provider.

Completing a GEO review does not guarantee indexing, citations, rankings, traffic, or inquiries. It gives teams a way to separate access, content, evidence, and answer outcomes, then identify what needs correction and what still needs time and retesting.

## Practical steps

Confirm that priority pages are stable and readable. Check crawl controls, sitemaps, canonical links, and the important text in the initial HTML. Review whether the brand, audience, deliverables, operating conditions, and limitations are stated plainly on the page.

Use a fixed group of buyer questions to inspect each target platform. Record source presence, source identity, and description accuracy separately. When a gap is found, change only content supported by reviewed evidence and keep before-and-after records.

## Verification checklist

- Important service pages are reachable through internal links and return readable text.
- Brand, service, deliverable, and boundary statements remain clear when a passage is read on its own.
- Consequential claims have nearby public support or reviewed first-party evidence.
- Chinese and English pages answer buyer questions in their own language.
- Each retest records the platform, date, question, response, and cited URLs.
- Indexing, citation, accurate description, and downstream visits are measured separately.
