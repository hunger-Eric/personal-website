## Direct answer

This article uses an operational definition. SEO mainly manages how a page performs in search discovery, presentation, and visits. GEO mainly manages whether public facts appear in generated answers, remain accurate, and receive appropriate source support.

That boundary does not imply two separate algorithms. The same page and fact can contribute to both outcomes. The distinction is useful because it tells a team which work item to open, which observations to preserve, and who can accept the result.

Google says AI Overviews and AI Mode are built on its core ranking and quality systems. Established SEO practices still apply, and there is no separate technical requirement that a site must implement for these features. [Google's guide to generative AI search](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide) GEO is therefore better managed as content, evidence, and measurement work for generated answers than as a replacement ranking system.

[Why GEO still matters after SEO](/en/articles/why-geo-after-seo) covers the case for doing both. This article does not repeat that argument. It compares work units, metrics, ownership, and investment order.

## The operational differences

| Question | Common SEO focus | Common GEO focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What does the user see? | Search results, snippets, images, local results, and other search features | A composed answer supported by links to one or more sources |
| What is improved? | Pages, URLs, site structure, and the relationship between a query and a landing page | Stand-alone facts, claims, evidence passages, and relationships among brands, services, and sources |
| What outcome is observed? | Impressions, position, clicks, organic sessions, and on-site conversion | Accurate mentions, correct use of key facts, source support, and visits referred by AI products |
| What commonly fails? | Crawling, indexing, relevance, presentation in results, or landing-page experience | A page is retrieved but its key facts are used incorrectly, the wrong source appears, or public claims conflict |
| What work follows? | Crawl and index fixes, information architecture, internal links, titles, content, and intent coverage | Question coverage, claim boundaries, nearby support, entity clarity, cross-page consistency, and repeated answer tests |
| What proves progress? | Search Console, analytics, ranking records, and conversions | Platform reports, complete answers, cited URLs, factual accuracy, referrals, and downstream behaviour |

This is a division of work, not a hard technical border. SEO already deals with passages, entities, and authority. When a generated answer uses public web sources, the evidence still resolves to pages and URLs. The distinction becomes useful when a team decides what it is accepting as a finished result.

## Why the unit of optimisation changes

Traditional search commonly presents a set of page links first. A title, snippet, and URL help the user decide whether to visit. Teams naturally organise work around URLs. A service page targets a group of related searches, an article answers a distinct need, and internal links connect the set.

Within Google Search's generative features, the system retrieves material before composing an answer. Google describes the use of retrieval-augmented generation and query fan-out, which can break one request into related searches. [Google's guide to generative AI search](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide) One answer may take a capability statement from a service page, an implementation condition from documentation, and supporting evidence from a case record or policy page. Other platforms need to be evaluated against their own published documentation.

That changes the scale of a content review. A clear page topic is still necessary. Important facts within the page must also remain intelligible when separated from the surrounding copy. Who provides the service? Who is it for? What inputs are supported? Which actions require human approval? How is the result accepted? What limitations apply? If those details are spread across vague paragraphs, retrieving the page may still be insufficient for an accurate answer.

The original GEO paper treated content visibility inside generative answers as an object that could be tested and measured. [The GEO paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735) It is a research starting point, not an industry-wide ranking standard. Results from its benchmark should not be converted into a promise about citations for a commercial website.

## Two acceptance records for one page

Consider a hypothetical enterprise software company. This example does not describe a SolveReal Systems customer or completed project.

The company sells invoice-recognition software. Its product page is indexed and receives impressions for searches such as “invoice OCR.” The team opens two work items for the same page.

A buyer asks a more specific question.

> Which invoice-extraction service supports scanned Chinese invoices, allows finance staff to review results before they enter the ERP, and can run in the company's own environment?

The existing page says only that the service is accurate, automated, and flexibly deployed. Those phrases suggest a direction but do not answer the three buying questions. Which invoices are supported? Where does human review occur? What deployments does “flexible” actually include?

| Record | Inputs | Work | Acceptance evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SEO | Relevant queries, target URL, current impressions and clicks | Review page fit, result presentation, internal links, and the next step after the visit | Indexing status, query data, clicks, and on-site behaviour |
| GEO | Stable buyer questions, approved service conditions, target product | Add version and deployment conditions, then verify that the answer preserves the human-review requirement | Complete answers, actual sources, factual review, and referred visits |

Both records may lead to the same page edit, but they cannot share one completion rule. If product and delivery owners have not approved the scope of local deployment, the SEO record can continue observing page performance. The GEO record cannot expand “flexible deployment” into a firm public promise.

A higher ranking does not establish answer accuracy. A cited page does not establish a visit or an inquiry. The outcomes may influence one another, but evidence for one cannot substitute for evidence of another.

## Measurement is where confusion becomes expensive

SEO has established reporting conventions. Google Search Console commonly describes search performance through clicks, impressions, click-through rate, and average position. Analytics tools then show what happened after a person reached the site. [Google on Search Console and site analytics](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/monitor-debug/google-analytics-search-console)

GEO has no universal cross-platform “AI rank.” Each product exposes a different slice of performance.

In 2026, Google added a dedicated Generative AI performance report with visibility data for its generative search features, including pages, countries, devices, and dates. [Google's Generative AI performance report](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/06/gen-ai-performance-reports) Bing Webmaster Tools introduced an AI Performance public preview that reports citation counts, cited pages, and grounding queries. Microsoft warns that these aggregates do not represent rankings, authority, or the role a page played in one particular answer. [Bing AI Performance](https://blogs.bing.com/webmaster/February-2026/Introducing-AI-Performance-in-Bing-Webmaster-Tools-Public-Preview)

OpenAI's publisher guidance says allowing OAI-SearchBot supports inclusion in ChatGPT search. Referral links from ChatGPT search include `utm_source=chatgpt.com`, which gives site owners a way to observe visits. [OpenAI's publisher and developer FAQ](https://help-lb.openai.com/en/articles/12627856-publishers-and-developers-faq)

A useful GEO record separates at least four layers.

1. **Appearance** — Did the brand or page appear in the answer or source list?
2. **Representation** — Did the answer describe the audience, capability, and limitations correctly?
3. **Support** — Does the cited page actually support the associated claim?
4. **Downstream action** — Did the user visit, read a priority page, or make an inquiry?

“Mentioned by AI” collapses all four layers. An accurate mention may have no citation. A citation may accompany a factual error. A visit may come from a different question. [How to measure a GEO change](/en/articles/how-to-measure-geo-ai-citations) provides a fuller measurement model.

## Which work should come first?

| Symptom | Priority | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A priority page cannot be crawled or remains absent from the index | Technical SEO first | The page is not reliably available to retrieval systems |
| The page is indexed but earns almost no impressions for relevant searches | Review topic focus, search demand, internal links, and content quality | The page may not yet solve a real search need |
| The page earns impressions but few clicks | Review the title, snippet, promise, and fit with the query | This is a result-to-visit problem |
| The page receives organic visits, but AI answers misstate the service | Add GEO fact and answer review | Discoverability does not ensure that an answer uses the facts correctly |
| An answer cites an old article instead of the current service page | Review old content, internal links, dates, canonical choices, and public-fact consistency | The diagnosis may involve both SEO and content governance |
| The answer is accurate and sourced but sends no visit | Check whether the answer already completed the task, then review the invitation and landing page | No click alone does not prove GEO failure |
| Chinese and English answers treat one brand as two companies | Review entity names, language versions, and public profiles | International SEO and GEO consistency both matter |

If access and indexing are still uncertain, begin with an [AI-search visibility audit](/en/articles/ai-search-visibility-audit-geo). Once that foundation is stable, move the investigation into answer accuracy and source support.

## Record the shared foundation once

Stable URLs, readable main content, internal links, accurate titles, verified facts, and structured data that agrees with the page serve both SEO and Google's generative Search features. Google says a page must be indexed and eligible for a search snippet before it can appear as a supporting link in those AI features. [Google on AI features and websites](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)

A team does not need two copies of that technical checklist. Shared problems enter one website backlog, while SEO and GEO retain separate acceptance records. GEO additionally preserves buyer questions, complete answers, actual sources, and factual review. The foundation is covered in [Why GEO still matters after SEO](/en/articles/why-geo-after-seo), so it is not repeated here.

## How the team can divide responsibility

An SEO or website owner manages crawling, indexing, site structure, and search performance. A content owner turns real needs into questions the page can answer and keeps important passages clear outside their original context. A business owner approves capabilities, conditions, and the boundaries of any public promise. An analyst preserves search data, generated answers, and records of on-site behaviour.

One person may hold several roles, but the right to approve a claim should remain explicit. A content team can discover that the site never answers whether local deployment is supported. It should not invent the answer. GEO puts facts into public answers, which means it also puts business commitments into a public setting. Material claims need approval from someone who understands the delivery conditions.

One shared question set can start the review, while the resulting records remain separate. The SEO owner records page and query performance. The GEO owner records answers and sources. The business owner decides whether the representation is accurate. When something changes, the team can find both the evidence and the person authorised to correct it.

## Three recurring mistakes

### Treating GEO as bulk FAQ production

Turning every long-tail question into a page can produce repetition and weak content. Page count does not equal fact coverage. Identify the decisions a buyer needs to make, then place each answer in the service page, documentation, case record, or article best suited to own it.

### Treating citation count as the business result

A citation shows that a page became a source in an observed answer. It does not establish that the answer was accurate, that a person visited, or that an inquiry followed. Each layer needs its own evidence.

### Combining every outcome into one GEO score

Ranking, citation, factual accuracy, visits, and inquiries carry different meanings. Compressing them into one number hides where a problem occurred. A summary dashboard can be useful, but the original fields and evidence should remain available.

## A practical four-week sequence

In week one, establish a baseline. Select one important service and ten to twenty real buying questions. Record search queries, landing pages, answers on target products, source URLs, and factual errors.

In week two, fix entry problems. Resolve crawling, indexing, canonical, internal-link, and main-content issues. If no technical failure exists, do not invent a separate infrastructure project in the name of GEO.

In week three, complete the decision facts. Ask the responsible owners to approve the audience, inputs, deliverables, human review, data handling, limitations, and acceptance criteria. Publish only reviewed facts and update affected languages and older pages.

In week four, retest the original questions. Record search impressions and clicks, answer accuracy, source support, and referred visits separately. When the sample is small, preserve the observations without presenting one movement as a stable trend.

At the end, the team should have a question set, page set, fact gaps, and a repeatable baseline. That is more actionable than an undefined “GEO score.”

## Boundaries

This guide concerns public-web visibility in search results and generated answers. Platforms use different indexes, crawler policies, retrieval methods, and interfaces. Google's rules cannot stand in for Bing, OpenAI, or another provider's current documentation.

SEO and GEO changes do not guarantee indexing, rankings, citations, traffic, or inquiries. Platform reports prove only the fields they cover. Assessing commercial value also requires site behaviour, lead quality, and the real sales process.

## Acceptance checklist

- The SEO work item names its target queries, URL, and search-performance evidence.
- The GEO work item names its target questions, platform, factual claims, and answer evidence.
- A shared technical problem creates one backlog item while the two acceptance records remain separate.
- Ranking, citation, accurate representation, visits, and inquiries are not compressed into a vague score.
- Material claims have a fact owner, and content staff do not expand delivery promises on their own.
- Chinese and English conditions are reviewed separately, with complete answers and actual sources preserved.
- Reports distinguish observations, inferences, and outcomes that remain unverified.
